## Simile
`When a comparison between two different objects is made because of some shared quality.`

For example,
+ He is as brave as a lion.
+ She is beautiful like a flower.
+ They are as talented as Einstein

1. The device only links the quality.
2. The two objects still maintain their own identity.

### Metaphor
When a direct comparison between two objects by calling one the other, is called a metaphor. It indicates some hidden shared quality that must be inferred.

For example,
+ He is a lion.
+ She is a flower.
+ He is Einstien.

1. The two objects' identies are merged into one despite being totally different.
2. The reason for the metaphor must be inferred and might depend on context.

### Hyperbole
It is a literary device that involves the use of intentional exaggeration to put emphasis on something or create humor.

For example,
- I am so hungry that I could eat a horse.
- There are like 10 million tasks I need to do.
- I have told you like a million times.

### Oxymoron
A figure of speech pairing two contradictory or opposite terms side by side to highlight paradoxes or create dramatic effects.

For example,
+ Deafening silence
+ Bitter sweet

### Irony
A contrast between expectation and reality.
- **Verbal Irony:** Saying one thing while meaning the exact opposite (similar to sarcasm). For example: Saying "What a lovely day" during a severe thunderstorm.
- **Situational Irony:** An event occurring that directly contradicts what was reasonably expected to happen. For example: A fire station burning down. 
- **Dramatic Irony:** A plot mechanism in literature or drama where the audience or reader knows a vital piece of information that the characters do not. For example: In a horror movie, the audience sees the killer hiding in a closet, but the main character steps inside unaware.

### Personification
Attributing human traits, emotions or behaviours in inanimate objects, animals or concepts.

For example.
+ The wind whispered to me.

### Alliteration
The repetition of same initial consonant sounds.

### Symbolism
The use of concrete objects, person, situation or action to represent an abstract idea or deeper meaning.

For example,
- A `Red Rose` representing romantic love.
- A `Dove` representing peace.
