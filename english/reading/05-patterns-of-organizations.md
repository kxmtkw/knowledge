# Patterns of Organization

> Supporting details and text is arranged by the author in a specific sequence called the Pattern of Organization.

## Types

+ `Chronological` Information and text is arranged in a time sequence where events occur one after another in the designated time.
+ `Define and Explain` Defines a term and then explains it. May use an example to explain it.
+ `Cause and Effect` Highlights an event and its consequence. Might have multiple causes or multiple effects.
+ `Comparison and Contrast` Highlights the similarities and differences in two different but related objects/concepts.
+ `Process` A step-by-step procedure of how an event occurs or how something must be done.
+ `Classification` Classifies objects/concepts in different categories.

## Conversion Strategy

Follow the `DTR` model
### Deconstruct

Deconstruct the passage to derive information out of it, stripping away any structural signals. The text is reduced to a neutral set of information.

+ `Entities` Nouns, objects, concepts ($A, B$)
+ `Attributes` Properties, traits, measurements ($T_1, T_2$)
+ `Relations` Causal links ($A \rightarrow B$), temporal sequences ($T_1 \rightarrow T_2$), or categorizations ($A \in C$).
### Translate

Map the underlying relations to match the target's required structure.

| **Target Pattern**           | **Required Primary Axis**            | **Transformation Rule**                               |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Chronological**            | Time ($t_0, t_1, t_2$)               | Sort entities/relations along a strict timeline.      |
| **Cause & Effect**           | Causality ($X \rightarrow Y$)        | Bind entities into directional cause-effect nodes.    |
| **Comparison & Contrast**    | Shared Attributes ($P_1, P_2$)       | Pivot entities into a matrix of identical traits.     |
| **Classification**           | Taxonomy ($C_1, C_2$)                | Group entities into mutually exclusive subsets.       |
| **Process**                  | Dependencies ($S_1 \Rightarrow S_2$) | Convert time or causality into actionable steps.      |
| **Definition & Explanation** | Identity ($X \equiv Y + P$)          | Anchor one primary entity, then stack its properties. |

### Reframe

Once the underlying relations have been translated, convert the the information back into a passage, using the structural signals of the target pattern.

### Summary

1. Take out the information from the passage, getting rid of any structural signals.
2. Capture the relations of the information and translate them to the relations of the target pattern.
3. Rewrite the passage using the new translated relations.
