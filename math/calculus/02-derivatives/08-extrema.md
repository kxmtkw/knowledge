# Extrema

By the *Extreme Value Theorem*, in any function $f$ with domain $[a, b]$, there exists $M$ and $m$ in the range of $f$ such that $m <= f(x) <= M$ for all $x$ in $[a,b]$.

In simple terms, every function has a maximum and minimum output value in a given domain.

> [!note] Local vs Absolute Extrema
> **Absolute extrema** is the highest/lowest point across the whole domain. 
> **Local extrema** is the highest/lowest point within a small neighbour hood.

## How to Find

### Local Extrema

1. Find the critical points and the endpoints (in case of closed domain) of the function.
2. Apply a derivative test.
	1. If the $y'$ changes from $+ve$ to $-ve$ then its a local maxima and if $y'$ changes from $-ve$ to $+ve$ then its a local minima. This is the *first derivative test*.
	2. If $y'' < 0$ then its a local maximum and if $y''>0$ then its a local minimum.

Follow the model: `CET` Critical Points, Endpoints, Test

### Absolute Extrema

1. Find the critical points and the endpoints (in case of closed domain) of the function.
2. Find $y$ for each of the candidate points.
3. The maximum and minimum value found are the absolute maxima and minima respectively.

Follow the model: `CEF` Critical Points, Endpoints, Find

> [!tip] Absolute Extrema
> No derivative test is required for the extrema points.

> [!warning] Endpoints
> Endpoints are not considered if the domain is open.
